"Prime [matter](/definitions/matter)" refers to matter as its own substance which is uncreated. Some, such as Aristotle, posit that prime matter is the pure reception of form which God acts upon; and, thusly, God and matter are coeternal.

Prime matter is impossible because it would imply a kind of [entity](/definitions/an-entity) which is not contigent upon anything for its existence but is not pure existence itself. If it has intrinsic existence, it must have no parts; and thusly its essence and existence are absolutely identical. If what it is and that which it is are identical and that which it is has self-subsistent existence, then what it is must be identical to 'existence existing'. If it is existence existing (viz., an [essent](/definitions/an-essent)), then it is absolutely realized in intelligibility with what it is; which is the exact opposite of prime matter *by definition* (as that which purely receives intelligibility from a form). In other words, the pure existence of prime matter would imply it is pure actuality--pure [act](/definitions/act); and pure act is the exact opposite of prime matter by definition.

Thuslly, prime matter cannot exist.
